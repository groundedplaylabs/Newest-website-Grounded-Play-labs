Functional Pretend Play

Play kitchens, wooden market sets, doctor kits, and tea parties are far more than fun pastimes—they are the natural laboratory where children test out real-world roles, practice sequence of events, and build deep emotional empathy.

The Science of Pretend Play

"When a child feeds a stuffed animal or slices a wooden tomato for you, they are demonstrating perspective-taking, motor planning, and joint attention—fundamental building blocks of conversational fluency and emotional regulation."

— Jessica Lewis, MA, BCBA · Founder

Core Developmental Skills Fostered:

Functional Communication
Theory of Mind & Empathy
Daily Life Task Sequencing
Medical & Routine Desensitization

Curated Recommendations for Home & Classroom

Screen-free, durable, open-ended materials recommended by our behavioral therapists.

Toy Item 01

Wooden Play Kitchen & Cutting Foods

Realistic Velcro or magnet-connected wooden vegetables, a sturdy wooden knife, and small cooking pots.

Skill Focus: Bilateral hand integration, slicing actions, multi-step sequencing ("first wash, then cook, then eat").
Toy Item 02

Pediatrician & Doctor Check-up Kit

Stethoscope, thermometer, fabric bandages, and tongue depressor in a soft carrying pouch.

Skill Focus: Desensitizing doctor visits, asking questions ("Where does it hurt?"), and expressing empathy.
Toy Item 03

Farmer's Market Stand & Register

Wooden crates with produce, cloth shopping bags, and a simple bell cash register.

Skill Focus: Social greetings ("Hello!", "Thank you"), exchange of items, categorization, and vocabulary expansion.
Toy Item 04

Wooden Tea Set & Picnic Basket

Teapot with removable lid, small wooden cups, spoons, and wooden cookies for shared meals.

Skill Focus: Pouring control, offering items to others, requesting ("More please"), and polite reciprocal play.
Toy Item 05

Doll or Stuffed Animal Bedtime Kit

Soft blanket, small brush, wooden toothbrush, and a simple bedtime storybook.

Skill Focus: Rehearsing night routines, soothing behaviors, emotional naming ("teddy is sleepy"), and caretaking.
Toy Item 06

Community Helper & Dress-Up Props

Mail carrier bag, chef hat, firefighter badge, or tool belt with soft wooden tools.

Skill Focus: Flexible thinking, stepping into different community roles, and collaborative pretend narratives.

Parent Coaching: How to Join Pretend Play

Simple behavioral adjustments you can make at home to invite communication without turning playtime into a quiz.

1. Follow Their Lead & Imitate

Rather than directing the story ("Make the soup now!"), watch what your child is doing and copy it with your own toy. When you imitate them, it builds immediate rapport and shared attention.

2. The "Playful Sabotage" Strategy

Give your child a teapot with the lid tightly stuck, or hand them an empty cup without a spoon. These fun, natural obstacles create motivating moments for spontaneous requests and problem-solving.

3. Narrate Instead of Interrogating

Try replacing constant questions ("What color is this apple?") with descriptive sportscaster commentary: "Mmm, crisp green apple! Chop, chop, chop!" It lowers pressure and models natural rich vocabulary.

4. Introduce Gentle Unexpected Twists

If your child repeats the exact same script every time, gently introduce a silly surprise: "Oh no, a dinosaur is coming to our picnic! Can we give him a cookie?" This builds cognitive flexibility.
