Outside Play

Balls, giant bubble wands, sidewalk chalk obstacle courses, and balance bikes provide essential vestibular input, gross motor strength, and joyful opportunities for shared attention in California sunshine.

The Science of Sensory Outdoor Play

"Outdoor play regulates the nervous system like nothing else. Proprioceptive 'heavy work'—pushing, pulling, balancing, running—naturally down-regulates anxiety and resets a child's focus for learning and speech."

— Jessica Lewis, MA, BCBA · Founder

Core Developmental Skills Fostered:

Vestibular & Sensory Regulation
Bilateral Balance & Coordination
Joint Visual Gaze & Tracking
Spontaneous Vocal Requests ("Pop!")

Parent Coaching: Maximizing Outdoor Play

Tips from Jessica Lewis, MA, BCBA on using outdoor movement for emotional regulation.

1. Use "Ready, Set... [Pause]"

Before blowing a bubble or pushing a swing, say in an upbeat rhythm: "Ready... Set..." and wait 3–4 seconds with expectant eye contact. This builds natural anticipation and encourages your child to say "Go!" or make eye contact.

2. Heavy Work for Restless Energy

If your child is crashing into furniture or having a hard transition indoors, take them outside for 10 minutes of heavy work: pushing a wheelbarrow of dirt, carrying river rocks, or pulling a wagon. It calms the central nervous system.

3. Draw Pathways on the Driveway

Draw a chalk obstacle course: a squiggly line to walk on tip-toes, 3 stars to jump on, and a big circle to spin in. Following simple motor sequences translates directly to classroom transitions.

4. Respect Nature Pace

Sometimes outdoor play isn't fast-paced sports—it's stopping to watch an ant for five minutes. Sit with your child in their fascination; shared stillness builds powerful social connection.
